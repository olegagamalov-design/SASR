Self-consistent re-audit of the p–11B stochastic-adaptive spheromak reactor

Closure hierarchy, corrected operating point, and multi-surface network drive

Oleg Agamalov · Independent Researcher, Ukraine

Under review ORCID 0000-0002-0330-8268 Zenodo audit suite
Abstract

A hierarchy of five closures is applied sequentially to the stochastic-adaptive spheromak reactor concept for aneutronic p–11B fusion. The audit shows that the operating point published earlier is over-pressured at β = 2.8, and that the extreme decoupling ratio Θ = Ti/Te ≈ 9 is not self-consistent: because ion–electron equilibration power scales as (Ti − Te)/Te3/2, minimising Te maximises the drain, and the electron ledger self-selects Θ ≈ 3. The corrected concept nevertheless retains a feasible region, and ignites within flowing-lithium wall limits. The two-condition Alfvénic targeting scheme — which corrects an error in the resonance condition of earlier work — generalises to a network of nine low-order rational surfaces driven by a frequency comb with phase-interleaved firing order. Four sharply-posed claims define the remaining research programme.

Five closures

An ordered sequence of physical constraints, each implemented as an executable audit. A design that survives makes claims a referee can recompute; a design that fails identifies precisely which physics must change.

I

Beta, field, density

Can the operating point be confined at all? Kinetic against magnetic pressure.

II

Transport and per-pass burn

The equilibration drain measured against fusion income, and what one proton pass returns.

III

The electron ledger

Te as an output of radiative equilibrium rather than a free parameter.

IV

Radiation accounting

Radiated power is a loss unless something demonstrably pays to sustain it.

V

First-wall loading

The constraint that removes the seductive high-power corner.

Three errors, stated plainly

  • The published operating point summed to a kinetic pressure 2.8× the magnetic pressure. No configuration can hold that plasma, and the discrepancy propagated silently through every downstream number.
  • The deposition condition was stated as k·B = 0. That condition belongs to the targeted tearing mode, not to the injected wave — which must retain finite parallel wavenumber at the same surface, or there is no Alfvén resonance at all.
  • An early version of this audit credited recovered radiation to the output with no input term resupplying the electrons, inflating the gain from 6.9 to 24. Recorded here so others may avoid it.

The nine-line drive

A single surface is a one-cylinder engine. Interleaving the crash phases of nine surfaces divides the per-crash energy, renders ion heating quasi-continuous, and holds the Chirikov overlap below unity by schedule rather than by amplitude.

One honest limitation: the layer width grows toward the axis — 25 mm at q = 5/7 — because the gradient of the local resonance frequency flattens there. The innermost surfaces are the least surgically addressable.

Four pillars

Everything assumed rather than derived, with the route to establishing each. Deliberately, all four are falsifiable.

Reactivity enhancement F

3–5 required

Fold the reconnection outflow spectrum against the measured cross-section with a bi-Maxwellian formalism. The 675 keV resonance is the physical basis.

Net synchrotron factor fsync

≤ 3×10−3

The single crudest input of the audit, with a cliff near 1.5×10−2. Needs a transparency calculation combined with measured liquid-lithium reflectivity at millimetre wavelengths and 23 T.

Confinement time τE

≥ 5 s

The concept's central falsifiable claim. The τE ∝ B² scaling has never been verified against any plasma; it is testable at SSPX scale with the 0.54 MHz single-line scheme.

Beta stability

0.5–0.6

Asserted from compact-toroid precedent rather than demonstrated for this configuration. An oblate flux conserver plus the firing-order schedule is the proposed stabilisation mechanism.
