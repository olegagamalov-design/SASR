Stochastic-Adaptive
Synchronisation of Rational Surfaces

Real-time phase locking of 3D-field drives to the natural frequencies of rational surfaces, under the stochastic frequency noise of real plasmas: a verified FPGA controller, a quantified feasibility criterion, and an audited D–T spherical-tokamak reference point. The programme began as the Stochastic-Adaptive Spheromak Reactor; it kept the name SASR and the methods that survived the 2026 audits.

Results Publications

The programme now

Three parts, each with its own evidence. None of them is a claim about plant power.

Control layer

A multi-harmonic resonance tracker for 3D-field coils. Each channel locks a drive to the resonance of a rational surface by its response phase, and reads out the natural frequency and damping rate in real time. The VHDL is bit-true to the MATLAB model and closes timing on an industrial-class FPGA. A lock on a real plasma needs an experimental partner.

Feasibility criterion

Whether such a lock is possible is set by the noise of the surface's natural frequency, and only in the band between the loop bandwidth and the linewidth. The model gives a threshold: lock when the in-band jitter is below 0.39 of the damping rate. It is a model result, not yet tested against plasma data.

Reference plant

A STEP-class D–T spherical tokamak with gyrotron current drive (S1), held in one master parameter file. It is the benchmark the control layer is measured against. The SASR mechanisms that would have changed its power balance were tested and closed.

Reference point S1

A 0D/1D point with a Grad–Shafranov/bootstrap equilibrium, sourced current-drive efficiency and sourced wall limits. All values are model results. Qeng is 3.05–4.42 over an ECCD efficiency ζ of 0.25–0.40; the table uses ζ = 0.30.

Gross electric over recirculating power, with ηth 0.31, ηdrv 0.51 and housekeeping fhk 0.05. The required confinement is H98 = 1.19, against 1.4 demonstrated in a spherical tokamak. What has been computed

Controller

Three NCO lock-in channels at 50 kHz. The generated VHDL matches the bit-true MATLAB reference sample for sample (0 mismatches in 3 × 50 000 samples, RTL simulation). On a Zynq-7010 (Vivado, placed and routed) it uses 15 DSP blocks and 60 % of the LUTs, and meets timing at 5 MHz, one clock per sample. In a closed loop through an emulated 8–16-bit ADC/DAC with up to 50 µs delay, the lock holds within 0.1 % of the linewidth once the delay is calibrated; without calibration the delay shifts the lock by tan(ωτ) half-linewidths.

Feasibility

A zero-slip lock to a tearing-stable surface needs s = σband/A < 0.39, where σband is the rms natural-frequency jitter between the loop bandwidth and the linewidth A; the linewidth readout is biased by 1.48 s². A sufficient condition for any correlation time is ρQ < 0.57 (ρ: relative jitter, Q: resonance quality). At reactor Lundquist numbers Q reaches 103–5×104 on S1.

Closed: current drive by relaxation events

Helicity balance on closed surfaces forbids an edge source from driving core current, axisymmetric or helical, and events above about 100 Hz are filtered by the plasma response. Reconnection events are not a current-drive or heating route on S1.

Closed: proton–boron-11 as a reactor fuel

With electron temperature from power balance and a sourced first-wall limit of 1 MW m−2, no variant reaches Qeng = 1 even for unlimited confinement: upper bounds 0.19 (spheromak) and 0.41–0.94 (FRC).

Stability

The Mercier interchange limit follows the sign of dι/dρ. In the relaxed spheromak state, where ι rises, βcrit ≈ 0.035; where ι falls, as in a tokamak or spherical torus, no interchange limit appears. This, with the fuel result, moved the reference plant to a spherical tokamak.

Where the work stands

From a reactor claim to a control result

The first published operating point (PPCF 2026) was over-pressured (β = 2.77) and is corrected; the later p–11B point at β = 0.57 sits sixteen times above the relaxed-spheromak interchange limit and above every sourced wall limit. Those numbers no longer describe the programme, and the earlier preprints that use them are superseded by the master parameter file.

  1. Dec 2025 – Jan 2026Stochastic-Adaptive Spheromak Reactor: conceptual design and first preprints on stochastic-adaptive control of reconnection events.
  2. 2026A proton–boron-11 spheromak operating point is published (PPCF 68 045016) and later corrected (corrigendum; the over-pressure β = 2.77 removed).
  3. Sep – Oct 2026Closure-hierarchy audits and a master parameter file: no proton–boron-11 variant reaches Qeng = 1 at sourced wall limits; the relaxed-spheromak interchange limit is β ≈ 0.035.
  4. Nov 2026The reference plant moves to a merging-formed D–T spherical tokamak (S1); current drive by relaxation events is closed; the FPGA resonance tracker is verified through RTL and synthesis; the programme is renamed Stochastic-Adaptive Synchronisation of Rational surfaces.

What remains is narrower and checkable: a controller verified from the MATLAB model through RTL simulation and synthesis, a criterion that says when it can lock, and a reference plant that does not depend on either. The next step is a lock on a real plasma signal with an experimental partner.

Scope and limitations

  • S1 is a 0D/1D reference point with a fixed-boundary equilibrium; no 3D transport or stability simulation has been performed.
  • The feasibility threshold is calibrated on Ornstein–Uhlenbeck jitter; real rotation noise may differ.
  • The controller is verified in simulation, RTL and synthesis; it has not yet acted on a plasma.
  • Lock-in and phase-locked tracking are standard techniques; the contribution is their application to 3D-field coils and the criterion.
  • The work is single-author and unfunded; it has not had the benefit of an experimental group's scrutiny.
