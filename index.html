Stochastic-Adaptive
Spheromak Reactor

A compact fusion concept in which pulsed magnetic reconnection is treated as a driven, controllable heating mechanism rather than a loss channel — paired with aneutronic proton–boron-11 fuel and a liquid-lithium first wall.

Operating point Publications

Three commitments

Each buys something and costs something. The concept is defined by the trades, not by any single component.

Driven reconnection

Reconnection events deposit energy preferentially into ions, following the Yamada 2:1 ion-to-electron partition. Treated as a Kramers escape problem, the event rate becomes a control variable rather than an instability — at the cost of requiring an active control loop whose statistics are so far validated only in simulation.

Aneutronic p–11B

No tritium breeding, no 14 MeV neutron wall load, no activation inventory. The price is a reactivity peak near 600 keV and bremsstrahlung losses severe enough that the electron temperature, not the ion temperature, becomes the governing design constraint.

Liquid-lithium wall

A flowing first wall removes the erosion-lifetime question and provides a renewable plasma-facing surface, at the cost of MHD pumping losses and an impurity budget that must be held tight enough not to reopen the radiation problem above.

The operating point

Self-consistent rather than aspirational. The electron temperature is solved from radiative equilibrium, which fixes the temperature ratio Θ and with it the whole point — the cooling race between fusion heating and bremsstrahlung has a window, and these numbers sit inside it. Move one and the others follow.

What has been computed

Stability

The Mercier interchange limit is controlled by the sign of dι/dρ. Where ι rises — the relaxed spheromak state — the limit is βcrit ≈ 0.035. Where ι falls, as in a tokamak or spherical torus, no interchange limit appears at all.

Self-consistency

A five-closure audit found the published operating point over-pressured at β = 2.77 — kinetic pressure nearly three times the magnetic. Enforcing pressure balance cut the density roughly fivefold, and solving Te from radiative equilibrium rather than assuming it relaxed the decoupling ratio from Θ ≈ 9 to the Θ = 3.0 the electron ledger selects for itself.

Power balance

At the corrected point the reactor ignites: alpha channeling sustains the ion channel while external drive falls to a 107 MW trigger, 2% of the 5.33 GW fusion power. Engineering gain is 4.9, with a first-wall load of 38.8 MW m−2 against a 40 MW m−2 flowing-lithium limit — inside the bound, but not by much.

Fuel selection

D–3He was evaluated and set aside on supply grounds: world 3He availability runs to roughly 10–20 kg per year against a requirement near 100 kg per year for a single gigawatt plant.

Control

Nine low-order rational surfaces are addressed simultaneously by a 0.44–1.04 MHz drive comb from a single antenna, with a phase-interleaved firing order that holds the Chirikov overlap below unity by schedule rather than by amplitude. The 325 ns FPGA pipeline updates roughly once per RF cycle. Validated in closed-loop benchmark and in hardware-in-the-loop on a Zynq XC7Z010 board — which verifies the controller, not its response to a real plasma.

Where the work stands

One structural incompatibility

The audited operating point requires β = 0.57, and the audit itself lists β stability at 0.5–0.6 as an assumed pillar rather than a derived result. For a relaxed spheromak, where the transform profile rises, Mercier analysis puts the interchange limit at βcrit ≈ 0.035. That is a factor of roughly 16 — not a margin to be engineered away, and not a numerical detail. It is a structural conflict between the operating point the power balance demands and the relaxed-state equilibrium.

Three routes remain open: a non-relaxed transform profile, a substantially higher field, or a device scale near 2 m. None has yet been shown to close the gap. It is stated here because the result is structural, and a concept page that omitted it would be advertising rather than research.

Scope and limitations

  • Confinement modeling is 0D/1D. No 3D transport simulation has been performed.
  • The scaling of Eq. (1) is derived, not measured. Its normalisation is unverified.
  • All plasma results are simulation-only. No device exists.
  • Hardware-in-the-loop testing validates the control electronics and timing, not the plasma response they would act on.
  • The work is single-author and unfunded; it has not had the benefit of an experimental group's scrutiny.
